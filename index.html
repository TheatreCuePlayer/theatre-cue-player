Show Control

  • One cue list, one GO button — a workflow a student learns in a rehearsal.
  • Multi-layer playback with overlapping audio instances.
  • Automation: auto-follow, auto-continue and infinite loops.
  • Fade cues, plus FADE ALL and STOP ALL for when something goes wrong.
  • Hotkeys for instant-fire sound effects.
  • Oversize transport and high-contrast mode for a dark booth.

Video & Projection

  • Second-screen output — clean full-screen projection, black between cues.
  • Projection mapping: corner-pin warp and masking onto set pieces and scrims.
  • Video cues fired from the same list as your audio — fades, loops, trimming, playback rate.
  • Text cues: titles, supertitles, crawls and rolls.

Shows That Travel

  • Your storage, your choice — a plain folder, or your own Google Drive.
  • Cue sheets as a printable PDF, or a Google Sheet for the stage manager.
  • Zip a whole show, media included, in one step.
  • A real sound editor built in — EQ, reverb, delay, noise reduction, pitch and tempo change. Send the modified copy right back to your cue list.
  • Works offline once a show is open, so a dropped network can’t stop a performance.
  • Help Mode locks the cue list so a student can’t edit it by accident.

How it uses Google Drive

Google Drive is optional. The extension works completely without it — in folder mode it never contacts Google, never asks you to sign in, and makes no network connections at all, which matters in districts where Drive is blocked.

If you do connect it, shows are saved to your own Drive so a cue list follows you from the classroom to the booth, and cue sheets can be exported straight to Google Sheets. It asks for a single per-file permission (drive.file) which lets it read and write only the show files it creates and the cue sheets it makes for you. It cannot see anything else in your Drive, and there is no Theatre Cue Player server — nothing you make is sent to the developer. Full detail is in the privacy policy.

Built in a classroom, for a classroom

Theatre Cue Player started as a way to teach a room full of students the skills, terminology and workflow of professional show control — without a professional budget, and without a professional learning curve.

That shapes the whole thing. Cue types, fades, follows, geometry, warping: they are named the way the industry names them, on purpose, so a student who runs a season here can sit down in front of professional software and recognise what they are looking at. The training is the point. The fact that it will also run your show is what makes the training real.

Where this leads

The vocabulary here is the industry’s vocabulary. When your students move on, these are the rooms they walk into — and they will already know what the words mean.

QLab

The standard for theatrical sound, video and lighting cues. Mac only. If a student learns one professional tool, it is usually this one.

Show Cue Systems

Theatrical sound cue playback for Windows, and a long-standing alternative to QLab for programmes that are not on Macs.

Isadora

Interactive media and projection for live performance, built around a visual patching workflow.

Resolume

Projection mapping and live video, widely used in concert and event work.

Ableton Live

Music production and sound design. Where original underscoring and effects tend to get made.
